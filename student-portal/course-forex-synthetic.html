Module 1 — Two Markets, One Skill Set

Forex and synthetic indices share one core skill: reading price and managing risk. Forex trades real currencies driven by economics, Monday to Friday. Synthetic indices are simulated, run 24/7 and ignore news.

Which should you trade?

If you can only trade in the evening or weekends, synthetics fit your time. If you like following economic stories and have set sessions, forex fits. Many traders master one first, then add the other.

Common rules for both

Trade with the higher-timeframe trend, mark zones, confirm with candles, risk 1%, journal every trade. Start on a demo account in both.

Worked example

A trader works 9 to 5. He trades forex during the London/New York overlap on his lunch break and Volatility 25 for 90 minutes in the evening, using the same strategy and same 1% risk on both.

Key takeaways
  • Same discipline, two different markets
  • Choose by your available time
  • Demo first

Module 2 — How the Forex Market Works

Forex (foreign exchange) is the global market where one currency is exchanged for another. It is open 24 hours a day, Monday to Friday, because trading passes from Sydney to Tokyo to London to New York.

Currency pairs

Every trade involves two currencies. In EUR/USD the first is the base and the second the quote; the price tells you how many dollars one euro costs. If you buy EUR/USD you expect the euro to rise against the dollar; if you sell you expect it to fall. Majors (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, NZD/USD) have the tightest spreads. Minors (EUR/GBP, GBP/JPY) and exotics cost more to trade.

Pips, lots and spread

A pip is the standard price step (0.0001 for most pairs, 0.01 for JPY pairs). A standard lot is 100,000 units (about $10 per pip on EUR/USD), a mini lot 0.10 and a micro lot 0.01 (about $0.10 per pip). The spread is the difference between buy and sell price; it is the broker's fee and you start every trade slightly negative.

Leverage and margin

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small deposit. At 1:100, margin for 1 lot of EUR/USD at 1.0850 is 100,000 × 1.0850 ÷ 100 = $1,085. Leverage magnifies profits and losses equally; most account blow-ups come from oversized positions, not from bad analysis.

Trading sessions

London (about 08:00–16:00 GMT) and New York (about 13:00–21:00 GMT) are the most active, and the overlap (13:00–16:00 GMT) has the best liquidity and biggest moves. Asian sessions are calmer and favour JPY, AUD and NZD pairs.

Worked example

Buy 0.10 lot EUR/USD at 1.0850 with spread 1.2 pips. Price rises to 1.0870 (+20 pips). Profit = 20 pips × $1 per pip = $20, minus the spread cost of about $1.20. If price had fallen to 1.0830 you would have lost $20 plus spread.

Key takeaways
  • Know base vs quote, pip, lot, spread and margin before placing a trade
  • Trade the London/New York overlap when liquidity is highest
  • Leverage is a risk multiplier, not free money
  • Always begin on a demo account

Module 3 — Market Structure & Price Action

Price is the only thing every trader sees. Learn to read it before adding any indicator.

Trends and ranges

An uptrend makes higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL); a downtrend makes lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL). When price keeps bouncing between two levels it is ranging. A trend ends when the last higher low (or lower high) is broken — this is a break of structure (BOS).

Support, resistance and zones

Support is where buyers stepped in before; resistance is where sellers did. Draw them as zones, not single lines, and mark them first on the Daily and H4 charts. A level touched 3+ times is strong. Once broken, support often becomes resistance (and the reverse) — the flip zone.

Candlestick signals

Read candles at key levels only. A pin bar (long wick, small body) shows rejection. A bullish engulfing candle closes above the previous candle's body and signals buyers taking control; bearish engulfing is the opposite. A doji shows indecision. A candle pattern in the middle of nowhere means nothing.

Top-down analysis

Start on Daily to see the main trend, move to H4/H1 for zones, then use M15/M5 for the entry. Never take an M5 buy straight into a strong Daily resistance.

Worked example

GBP/USD is in a Daily uptrend. Price pulls back to a previous resistance level at 1.2700 that has flipped to support, and a bullish engulfing candle forms on H1. Entry buy at 1.2712, stop-loss below the zone at 1.2680 (32 pips), target the last high at 1.2776 (64 pips) = 1:2 risk-to-reward.

Key takeaways
  • Trade in the direction of the higher timeframe trend
  • Mark zones on higher timeframes, enter on lower ones
  • Candles matter only at key levels
  • A break of structure is the first warning that the trend is changing

Module 4 — Technical Analysis Tools

Indicators are calculated from price, so they lag. Use them to confirm what price structure already shows — never as the only reason to trade.

Moving averages

The 50 EMA and 200 EMA define trend: price above both with 50 above 200 is bullish. In a trend, price often pulls back to the 50 EMA — a popular entry area. Crossovers are slow in ranging markets, so only use them when structure confirms a trend.

RSI and MACD

RSI (14) measures momentum from 0–100: above 70 is overbought and below 30 oversold, but in strong trends it can stay there for long. Its best use is divergence: price makes a new high while RSI makes a lower high, warning of weakening momentum. MACD crossing its signal line shows momentum shifts.

Fibonacci retracement

Drawn from swing low to swing high (for a buy), it shows where a pullback may end: 38.2%, 50% and 61.8%. The best entries occur when a Fibonacci level lines up with a support zone and a moving average.

Confluence

Take a trade only when at least 3 independent things agree, for example: (1) higher-timeframe trend, (2) support zone, (3) confirmation candle. The more that agree, the higher the quality of the setup — and the fewer trades you take.

Worked example

USD/JPY trends up on H4 (price above 50 and 200 EMA). It pulls back to the 61.8% Fibonacci retracement, which sits exactly on the 50 EMA and an old support zone. RSI shows bullish divergence and a pin bar prints. That is four confirmations at one spot: a high-quality buy setup.

Key takeaways
  • Price structure first, indicators second
  • RSI divergence is more useful than overbought/oversold readings
  • Fibonacci works best inside a trend
  • Use 3+ confluences and skip the rest

Module 5 — Fundamental Analysis

Economic data and central-bank decisions drive currency strength over weeks and months, and cause sharp spikes at the moment they are released.

Interest rates

Currencies with higher or rising interest rates usually attract investment and strengthen. Learn to follow the Fed, ECB, BoE and BoJ: the tone of their statements often moves the market more than the rate change itself.

Key data releases

CPI (inflation), NFP (US jobs, first Friday of the month), GDP, retail sales and PMIs. A result better than forecast usually strengthens that currency; worse weakens it. The market trades the surprise versus the forecast, not the number itself.

Using the economic calendar

Check it every morning. High-impact (red) events can move price 50–100 pips in seconds with huge spreads. Beginners should avoid opening trades 30 minutes before and after, and should never hold a tight stop through NFP.

Risk sentiment

In risk-on markets investors buy AUD, NZD and stocks; in risk-off they run to USD, JPY and CHF. Sentiment explains why pairs sometimes move together.

Worked example

US CPI is forecast at 3.1% but prints 3.5%. Traders expect the Fed to keep rates high, so USD strengthens and EUR/USD drops 60 pips in minutes. A trader who waited for the spike to settle and then sold the pullback to a resistance zone avoided the chaos and traded a cleaner move.

Key takeaways
  • Trade the surprise, not the number
  • Check the calendar daily and avoid high-impact news as a beginner
  • Combine fundamentals (direction) with technicals (timing)

Module 6 — What Are Synthetic Indices?

Synthetic indices (for example, Deriv's) are simulated markets whose prices are generated by a certified random-number generator. They are not tied to any real asset, so they run 24/7, have no news events and are not affected by economic data.

Volatility indices

The number shows the average annualised volatility: Volatility 10 is the calmest, Volatility 75 and 100 the fastest. A '(1s)' version produces a new price every second. Higher volatility means larger candles, bigger opportunity and bigger risk.

Boom and Crash

Boom 500/1000 drifts down slowly with occasional sharp upward spikes; Crash 500/1000 drifts up with occasional downward spikes. On average one spike appears every 500 (or 1000) ticks, but the exact timing is random.

Step, Jump and Range Break

Step Index moves in fixed steps and is ideal for beginners. Jump indices have sudden price jumps at random intervals. Range Break trades inside ranges that periodically break out.

Advantages and risks

Advantages: always open, no news gaps, consistent behaviour. Risks: very fast moves, high leverage available, and constant availability tempting overtrading. Treat it as a high-risk activity; trade only money you can afford to lose.

Worked example

On a Volatility 75 chart, a 5-minute candle may move many times more than a typical EUR/USD candle. A trader used to forex lot sizes would lose far more than planned, which is why position size must be recalculated for every index.

Key takeaways
  • Know each index's behaviour before trading it
  • No news and 24/7 trading — choose set trading hours
  • Higher volatility number = smaller lot size

Module 7 — Platforms, Accounts & Contract Specifications

You trade synthetics on a broker platform such as Deriv MT5, Deriv Trader or Deriv's own web terminal. Always begin with the free demo account.

Opening and funding

Create your account (use the button on this page), verify your identity, open a demo account first (virtual funds) and a real account only when you are consistently following your rules. Use deposit methods available in your country and start with a small amount.

Charts and platforms

MT5 offers professional charts and indicators; TradingView gives cleaner charting for analysis. Use the same timeframes every day (H1 for bias, M5/M15 for entries).

Contract specifications

Each index has its own minimum lot, tick size and value per point. Look them up in the platform's contract specification before trading. Always size positions with: Lot = Risk $ ÷ (stop distance in points × value per point per lot).

Fees and execution

Check spreads and swap rules, avoid trading during platform maintenance, and keep a stable internet connection.

Worked example

You have a $500 demo account and want to risk 1% ($5). The platform shows a 40-point stop on your chosen index. If the value per point for 1 lot is $1, then Lot = $5 ÷ (40 × $1) = 0.125 lot, rounded down to the nearest valid size. Always check the real number on your platform.

Key takeaways
  • Begin on demo, then go live small
  • Read the contract specification of every index
  • Position sizing formula is the same for every market

Module 8 — Trading Volatility Indices

Volatility indices trend and range just like any other market. Treat structure first, indicators second.

Trend following

Use H1 for the bias (price above/below the 50 EMA, higher highs or lower lows) and M5/M15 for entries on pullbacks to the 50 EMA or a support/resistance zone. Trade with the trend.

Breakout and retest

When price breaks a key level with a strong candle, wait for it to return to retest the level; enter when it holds with a confirmation candle. This avoids many false breakouts.

When not to trade

Skip choppy, directionless price in the middle of a range, skip when you have lost your daily limit, and skip when you are tired or emotional. 24/7 availability does not mean you should trade 24/7.

Choosing your index

Beginners should start with Volatility 10, 25 or 50 or the Step Index; move to V75/V100 only after consistent profits, always with smaller lots.

Worked example

V50 trends up on H1 (above 50 EMA). Price breaks above a resistance zone, returns to retest it and prints a bullish engulfing candle on M15. Entry on the candle close, stop below the retest low, target 1:2. If price breaks back below the zone, the trade is invalid and the stop protects you.

Key takeaways
  • Trade with the higher-timeframe trend
  • Breakout + retest filters false moves
  • Start on lower-volatility indices

Module 9 — Boom & Crash Spike Strategy

Boom and Crash offer two types of moves: slow trends and sudden spikes. Beginners lose money by trying to guess the spike.

Trade the slow trend

On Boom, the slow drift is down; on Crash, up. Many traders trade with the drift and exit quickly instead of waiting for a spike, using small profits and tight management.

Spike trading

Spike traders enter small lots in the direction of the spike expecting it to happen soon, using strict stops. This has a low win rate and a large reward. It only works with discipline: tiny risk per attempt and a fixed number of attempts.

Spike warnings

Price exhaustion, repeated rejection from a level, and long runs in one direction increase the chance that a spike is near, but never guarantee it.

Rules that protect you

Never hold against the trend hoping for a reversal; never increase lot size after a loss; accept that a spike can occur right after you exit.

Worked example

Crash 500 trades down in a slow drift on M5. You sell on a pullback to resistance with a stop above the last high and target 1:2. The price drifts to target in 15 minutes. You close the trade; a spike candle appears afterwards. You protected yourself instead of holding through the spike.

Key takeaways
  • Trade the drift with the trend, exit quickly
  • Spike trading needs tiny risk and strict stops
  • No signal guarantees a spike

Module 10 — Building a Complete Trading Strategy

A strategy is a written set of rules that tells you exactly when to enter, where to place the stop, where to exit and how much to risk. If a rule is not written, it is a guess.

The five parts of a strategy

1) Market and timeframe, 2) Setup conditions, 3) Entry trigger, 4) Stop-loss and take-profit, 5) Risk per trade. Write them on one page.

Example: Trend Pullback strategy

Pairs: EUR/USD, GBP/USD. Bias: H4 price above 50 EMA and 50 above 200 EMA (buys only). Setup: pullback into the 50 EMA or the 50–61.8% Fib zone, at a support zone. Entry: bullish engulfing or pin bar on M15/H1. Stop-loss: below the pullback low. Take-profit: at least 1:2. Risk: 1%. Reverse everything for sells.

Risk-to-reward and win rate

With 1:2 you only need to win 33.4% of trades to break even. With 40% winners: expectancy = (0.40 × 2R) − (0.60 × 1R) = +0.2R per trade, so 100 trades would produce about +20R. Edge comes from rules + consistency, not from a high win rate.

Backtesting and forward-testing

Scroll back on the chart and record at least 100 trades using your exact rules (date, entry, stop, result in R). Then forward-test on demo for 4+ weeks. Only go live when results are positive and you followed the rules every time.

Worked example

Entry 1.0850, stop-loss 1.0830 (20 pips), take-profit 1.0890 (40 pips). Risk 1R = 20 pips. Ten trades: 4 winners (+2R each = +8R) and 6 losers (−1R each = −6R) = +2R net, even though you lost more trades than you won.

Key takeaways
  • Write the strategy on one page
  • Aim for minimum 1:2 reward-to-risk
  • Backtest 100 trades, then demo test for 4+ weeks
  • Consistency of execution matters more than the win rate

Module 11 — Risk Management & Trading Psychology

Professional traders think about what they can lose before what they can win. Surviving long enough to learn is the real goal.

The 1% rule and position sizing

Risk 1% (maximum 2%) of your account per trade. Formula: Lot size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Stop-loss in pips × pip value per lot). Never choose a lot size first and then place the stop where it 'fits'; the chart decides the stop, the stop decides the lot size.

Drawdown mathematics

Losses are harder to recover than they look: a 10% loss needs +11% to recover, 25% needs +33%, 50% needs +100%. This is why small, controlled risk matters.

Daily and weekly limits

Set a daily loss limit (for example 3%) and a maximum number of trades per day (for example 3). When you hit the limit, close the platform. Most large losses come on the day a trader tries to win it back.

The psychology traps

Fear makes you skip valid setups; greed makes you move take-profit or oversize; revenge trading happens after a loss; FOMO makes you chase price. Fix them with rules, a checklist before every trade, and a trading journal.

Worked example

Account $1,000, risk 1% = $10. Stop-loss 20 pips on EUR/USD ($10 per pip per standard lot). Lot size = $10 ÷ (20 × $10) = 0.05 lot. If stopped out you lose $10 (1%); if the 1:2 target hits you make $20 (2%). Ten losses in a row would cost about 9.6% — painful but survivable.

Key takeaways
  • Risk 1% per trade; the stop decides the lot size
  • Large drawdowns are very hard to recover
  • Daily loss limit protects you from emotional trading
  • Journal every trade: setup, result, emotion
