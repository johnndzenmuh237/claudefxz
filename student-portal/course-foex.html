1.1 What is Forex?

Forex (foreign exchange, FX) is the global, decentralised market where one currency is exchanged for another. It is the largest financial market in the world, with roughly $7 trillion traded every day, and it runs 24 hours a day from Monday to Friday. Importers, tourists, banks, governments and speculators all use it.

Currencies are always traded in pairs: you buy one and sell the other at the same time. The price of EUR/USD (for example 1.0850) says that 1 euro costs 1.0850 US dollars. If the euro gets stronger, the price rises; if the dollar gets stronger, it falls.

Simple example

A Cameroonian importer must pay a supplier $10,000. Over a month the exchange rate moves and the same payment costs more in local currency. A trader never needs the physical currency: the trader just bets on whether the rate will rise or fall, buys or sells a contract through a broker, and closes the trade for a profit or a loss.

1.5 Pips — what they are and how to calculate them

A pip (percentage in point) is the standard unit used to measure price movement. On most pairs it is the 4th decimal place (0.0001); on JPY pairs it is the 2nd decimal place (0.01). Many brokers also show a 5th decimal (a pipette, one tenth of a pip).

PairMovePipsHow
EUR/USD1.0850 → 1.087525 pips(1.0875 − 1.0850) ÷ 0.0001
GBP/USD (sell)1.2700 → 1.264060 pips profit(1.2700 − 1.2640) ÷ 0.0001
USD/JPY150.20 → 150.7050 pips(150.70 − 150.20) ÷ 0.01
Formulas

Pips = price difference ÷ pip size. Profit = pips × pip value × lots. For pairs where USD is the quote currency (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD), pip value = $10 per standard lot. For USD/JPY: pip value = (0.01 ÷ price) × 100,000 = about $6.67 per lot at 150.00.

Example

You buy 0.20 lot of EUR/USD at 1.0850 and close at 1.0900. Move = 50 pips. Pip value for 0.20 lot = 0.20 × $10 = $2. Profit = 50 × $2 = $100. If price had fallen to 1.0820 (30 pips) you would have lost 30 × $2 = $60.

1.6 Lot size — how it works and how to calculate it

A lot is the size of your trade. Bigger lot = more money per pip = more profit and more loss.

Lot typeLot sizeUnitsEUR/USD value per pip
Standard1.00100,000$10
Mini0.1010,000$1
Micro0.011,000$0.10
Lot size formula

Lot size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Stop-loss in pips × $10) for USD-quoted pairs. The stop-loss comes from the chart; the lot size is then calculated, never guessed.

Example 1 — correct

Account $1,000, risk 1% = $10, stop-loss 25 pips on EUR/USD. Lot = $10 ÷ (25 × $10) = 0.04 lot. If stopped out you lose $10.

Example 2 — dangerous

Account $500, trader opens 1.00 lot with a 20-pip stop. Risk = 20 × $10 = $200 = 40% of the account in one trade. Two losses and the account is almost gone.

Position value and margin: 0.10 lot of EUR/USD at 1.0850 controls 10,000 × 1.0850 = $10,850. At 1:100 leverage the margin held is about $108.50.

Traders earn from price differences. You do not need the market to go up: you can profit from falling prices by selling first.

Sara has $1,000 and risks 1% ($10) per trade with a 1:2 plan. In a month she takes 20 trades: 8 wins (+$20 each = $160) and 12 losses (−$10 each = −$120). Net +$40 (+4%) even though she lost more trades than she won. Profit comes from risk control, not from winning every time.

1.13 Trading rules to apply before every trade

Pre-trade checklist
  • Is the **higher time-frame trend** in my direction?
  • Is price at a **key support/resistance zone**?
  • Do I have a **confirmation candle**?
  • Is the **reward at least 2× the risk** (1:2)?
  • Is my risk **1% or less** and the lot size calculated?
  • Is my **stop-loss** placed beyond structure?
  • Is there **high-impact news** soon?
  • Am I **calm** (not angry, tired or chasing)?
  • Have I hit my **daily loss limit**? If yes, stop.
  • Is this trade in my **written plan**? If not, skip it.

1.14 Time frames and how they work

A time frame is how long one candle represents. A 1-hour (H1) chart draws one candle per hour. Different time frames show the same market at different zoom levels.

Time frameOne candle =Trader styleUse it for

Top-down example

Daily chart: EUR/USD is in an uptrend → H4: price pulls back to a support zone → M15: a bullish engulfing candle forms. You BUY on M15 because the higher time frames agree. If the Daily were in a downtrend, the same M15 buy would be a low-probability trade.

Rule

The higher time frame always wins. Analyse from the top down; enter from the bottom up.

1.15 Currency correlation

Correlation shows how two pairs move together, from +1 (move together) to −1 (move opposite). Typical values (they change over time):

PairsTypical relationshipWhat it means
EUR/USD & GBP/USDStrong positive (+0.8)Both rise/fall with the dollar
AUD/USD & NZD/USDStrong positive (+0.9)Almost the same trade
EUR/USD & USD/CHFStrong negative (−0.9)Buying one ≈ selling the other
USD/CAD & oil pricesNegativeHigher oil usually helps CAD
Hidden-risk example

You BUY EUR/USD, BUY GBP/USD and SELL USD/CHF, each at 1% risk. All three are really the same bet against the dollar, so one USD move can lose 3%. Treat correlated trades as one trade and split your risk, or pick the single best pair.

Risk Management

The skill that keeps you in the game long enough to become profitable.

2.1 What is risk management?

Risk management is the set of rules that control how much you can lose on one trade, in one day and in total. You cannot control what the market does, but you can fully control your position size, stop-loss and number of trades. Professionals think about protecting capital first and making profit second.

2.2 What to do and what NOT to do

✅ DO❌ DON'T
Risk 1% (max 2%) per tradeRisk 10–50% "to grow fast"
Always use a stop-lossTrade without a stop or remove it
Let the stop decide the lot sizePick a big lot first and place the stop to "fit"
Aim for at least 1:2 reward-to-riskTake 1:0.5 trades with a low win rate
Set daily and weekly loss limitsKeep trading after a bad day
Reduce size after a losing streakDouble size to "win it back"
Limit correlated tradesOpen EUR/USD, GBP/USD and AUD/USD all long
Keep a trading journalRely on memory

2.3 The 1% rule and position sizing

Formula

Lot size = (Account × Risk %) ÷ (Stop-loss in pips × Pip value per lot). On EUR/USD, 1 standard lot is worth about $10 per pip.

  1. 1Decide the account risk: 1% of the account.
  2. 2Find the stop-loss on the chart (beyond structure) and count the pips.
  3. 3Calculate lot size with the formula.
  4. 4Place entry, stop-loss and take-profit.
  5. 5Confirm: if the stop is hit, you lose only 1%.
Worked example

Account $2,000, risk 1% = $20. EUR/USD buy at 1.0850, stop at 1.0820 (30 pips). Lot = $20 ÷ (30 × $10) = 0.066 → 0.06 lot. If stopped, you lose about $18; if the 1:2 target (60 pips) is hit, you make about $36.

2.4 Risk-to-reward ratio

Risk : RewardWin rate needed to break evenComment
1 : 150%Hard to profit
1 : 1.540%OK
1 : 233.3%Recommended minimum
1 : 325%Excellent, fewer winners needed
Example

Entry 1.2700, stop 1.2670 (30 pips), target 1.2760 (60 pips) = 1:2. Even with only 4 winners in 10 trades: 4 × 2R − 6 × 1R = +2R profit.

The bigger a loss, the harder it is to recover:

Risk of ruin

10 losses in a row at 1% risk = −9.6% (survivable). At 10% risk the same streak = −65%. Even good strategies have losing streaks, so small risk is your insurance.

  1. 1Check daily loss limit and open risk.
  2. 2Confirm the setup meets the plan.

3.1 What is fundamental analysis?

Fundamental analysis studies the economic strength of a country to judge whether its currency should rise or fall. A strong economy with rising interest rates attracts investors, who buy the currency; a weak economy with falling rates does the opposite. It tells you the direction (WHY), while technical analysis tells you the timing (WHEN).

4.1 What is technical analysis?

Technical analysis studies past price movement to find high-probability entries. It rests on three ideas: (1) price reflects all information, (2) price moves in trends, (3) history tends to repeat. The main types are price action, chart patterns, indicators and Fibonacci/pivot tools.

4.2 Candlesticks: open, close, high, low

Each candle shows four prices for its time frame: the open, close, high and low. The thick body is the distance between open and close; the thin wicks show the extremes. A bullish candle closes above its open (buyers won); a bearish candle closes below its open (sellers won). Long wicks mean rejection; a big body means strong conviction.

4.4 Price action: trends, support and resistance

An uptrend makes higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL); a downtrend makes lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL); a range moves sideways between two levels. A trend is in danger when the last HL (or LH) is broken (break of structure).

  • Support = a zone where buyers stepped in before; resistance = where sellers did. Draw zones, not lines.
  • The more times a zone was respected, the stronger it is.
  • Broken resistance often becomes support (and vice versa) — the flip zone.
  • Trade pullbacks to the zone in the direction of the trend.
Example

EUR/USD uptrend: price breaks above 1.0900 resistance, pulls back to 1.0900 (now support), and a bullish pin bar forms. Buy at 1.0915, stop 1.0880 (35 pips), target 1.0985 (70 pips) = 1:2.

5.1 What is trading psychology?

Your strategy tells you what to do; psychology decides whether you actually do it. Trading psychology is how emotions, beliefs and habits affect your decisions. Physiology is how your body (sleep, stress, energy) affects your focus. Most traders do not fail because they lack a strategy — they fail because they cannot follow it under pressure.

5.4 The two cycles

Example story

Two traders both lose $20 (2%). Trader A closes the platform, writes the mistake in his journal and returns the next day. Trader B immediately doubles the lot size to recover and loses $120. One protected his capital and his mind; the other turned a small loss into a big one.

5.6 Daily discipline routine

  1. 1Before: sleep well, review the news calendar, mark levels, write down max trades and loss limit.
  2. 2Check mood: if angry, tired or stressed — do not trade today.
  3. 3During: follow the pre-trade checklist; one trade at a time; no moving the stop.
  4. 4After each trade: screenshot, record result in R and your emotion.
  5. 5End of day: stop at the limit; review mistakes, not just profit.
  6. 6Weekly: review the journal, pick one habit to improve.
Mindset rules
  • I accept that **any single trade can lose**.
  • I judge myself on **following the rules**, not on one result.
  • I never risk money I cannot afford to lose.
  • I protect capital first, profit second.
  • I am a business owner: consistency over excitement.
