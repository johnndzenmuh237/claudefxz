Module 1 — What Are Synthetic Indices?

Synthetic indices (for example, Deriv's) are simulated markets whose prices are generated by a certified random-number generator. They are not tied to any real asset, so they run 24/7, have no news events and are not affected by economic data.

Volatility indices

The number shows the average annualised volatility: Volatility 10 is the calmest, Volatility 75 and 100 the fastest. A '(1s)' version produces a new price every second. Higher volatility means larger candles, bigger opportunity and bigger risk.

Boom and Crash

Boom 500/1000 drifts down slowly with occasional sharp upward spikes; Crash 500/1000 drifts up with occasional downward spikes. On average one spike appears every 500 (or 1000) ticks, but the exact timing is random.

Step, Jump and Range Break

Step Index moves in fixed steps and is ideal for beginners. Jump indices have sudden price jumps at random intervals. Range Break trades inside ranges that periodically break out.

Advantages and risks

Advantages: always open, no news gaps, consistent behaviour. Risks: very fast moves, high leverage available, and constant availability tempting overtrading. Treat it as a high-risk activity; trade only money you can afford to lose.

Worked example

On a Volatility 75 chart, a 5-minute candle may move many times more than a typical EUR/USD candle. A trader used to forex lot sizes would lose far more than planned, which is why position size must be recalculated for every index.

Key takeaways
  • Know each index's behaviour before trading it
  • No news and 24/7 trading — choose set trading hours
  • Higher volatility number = smaller lot size

Module 2 — Platforms, Accounts & Contract Specifications

You trade synthetics on a broker platform such as Deriv MT5, Deriv Trader or Deriv's own web terminal. Always begin with the free demo account.

Opening and funding

Create your account (use the button on this page), verify your identity, open a demo account first (virtual funds) and a real account only when you are consistently following your rules. Use deposit methods available in your country and start with a small amount.

Charts and platforms

MT5 offers professional charts and indicators; TradingView gives cleaner charting for analysis. Use the same timeframes every day (H1 for bias, M5/M15 for entries).

Contract specifications

Each index has its own minimum lot, tick size and value per point. Look them up in the platform's contract specification before trading. Always size positions with: Lot = Risk $ ÷ (stop distance in points × value per point per lot).

Fees and execution

Check spreads and swap rules, avoid trading during platform maintenance, and keep a stable internet connection.

Worked example

You have a $500 demo account and want to risk 1% ($5). The platform shows a 40-point stop on your chosen index. If the value per point for 1 lot is $1, then Lot = $5 ÷ (40 × $1) = 0.125 lot, rounded down to the nearest valid size. Always check the real number on your platform.

Key takeaways
  • Begin on demo, then go live small
  • Read the contract specification of every index
  • Position sizing formula is the same for every market

Module 3 — Trading Volatility Indices

Volatility indices trend and range just like any other market. Treat structure first, indicators second.

Trend following

Use H1 for the bias (price above/below the 50 EMA, higher highs or lower lows) and M5/M15 for entries on pullbacks to the 50 EMA or a support/resistance zone. Trade with the trend.

Breakout and retest

When price breaks a key level with a strong candle, wait for it to return to retest the level; enter when it holds with a confirmation candle. This avoids many false breakouts.

When not to trade

Skip choppy, directionless price in the middle of a range, skip when you have lost your daily limit, and skip when you are tired or emotional. 24/7 availability does not mean you should trade 24/7.

Choosing your index

Beginners should start with Volatility 10, 25 or 50 or the Step Index; move to V75/V100 only after consistent profits, always with smaller lots.

Worked example

V50 trends up on H1 (above 50 EMA). Price breaks above a resistance zone, returns to retest it and prints a bullish engulfing candle on M15. Entry on the candle close, stop below the retest low, target 1:2. If price breaks back below the zone, the trade is invalid and the stop protects you.

Key takeaways
  • Trade with the higher-timeframe trend
  • Breakout + retest filters false moves
  • Start on lower-volatility indices

Module 4 — Boom & Crash Spike Strategy

Boom and Crash offer two types of moves: slow trends and sudden spikes. Beginners lose money by trying to guess the spike.

Trade the slow trend

On Boom, the slow drift is down; on Crash, up. Many traders trade with the drift and exit quickly instead of waiting for a spike, using small profits and tight management.

Spike trading

Spike traders enter small lots in the direction of the spike expecting it to happen soon, using strict stops. This has a low win rate and a large reward. It only works with discipline: tiny risk per attempt and a fixed number of attempts.

Spike warnings

Price exhaustion, repeated rejection from a level, and long runs in one direction increase the chance that a spike is near, but never guarantee it.

Rules that protect you

Never hold against the trend hoping for a reversal; never increase lot size after a loss; accept that a spike can occur right after you exit.

Worked example

Crash 500 trades down in a slow drift on M5. You sell on a pullback to resistance with a stop above the last high and target 1:2. The price drifts to target in 15 minutes. You close the trade; a spike candle appears afterwards. You protected yourself instead of holding through the spike.

Key takeaways
  • Trade the drift with the trend, exit quickly
  • Spike trading needs tiny risk and strict stops
  • No signal guarantees a spike

Module 5 — Step, Jump & Range Break and Trade Management

Each index has its own rhythm. A clear management plan matters more than the entry.

Step Index

Steps of constant size make it predictable and smooth; trade pullbacks to the 50 EMA on M5/M15 in the trend direction.

Jump and Range Break

On Jump indices, sudden jumps can hit stops; use wider stops with smaller lots. On Range Break, trade bounces inside the range and breakouts that hold on a retest.

Trade management

Take partial profit at 1R and move the stop to breakeven only after the structure confirms. Trail the remaining position behind new swing highs/lows. Never move your stop further away.

Backtesting each index

Backtest every index separately (at least 100 trades each). Their behaviour differs, so a strategy that works on V25 may not work on Crash 500.

Worked example

Entry at 1,000 with stop at 980 (20 points risk). At 1,020 (1R) close half the position and move the stop to 1,000 (breakeven). The rest reaches 1,040 (2R). Result: +0.5R from the first half + 1R from the second half = +1.5R instead of a possible loss.

Key takeaways
  • Partial profit and breakeven protect the account
  • Never widen a stop
  • Backtest each index separately

Module 6 — Risk Management & Discipline

Synthetic indices move quickly all day and night. Discipline is what keeps your account alive.

Position sizing

Risk 1% per trade (0.5% on V75/V100). Use the contract-specification formula every time.

Daily limits

Fixed daily loss (for example 3%) and maximum trades (for example 3). When reached, close the platform. No exceptions.

Avoid overtrading

24/7 markets create endless temptation. Choose a fixed trading window (for example 2 hours) and take only A+ setups.

The journal

Record index, setup, entry, stop, result in R, a screenshot and your emotion. Review it each week and remove your worst habit.

Worked example

Account $500. Risk 1% = $5 per trade, daily loss limit 3% = $15. After three losing trades (−$15) the trader stops for the day. Without the limit, chasing the loss with bigger lots could turn a −3% day into a −15% day.

Key takeaways
  • Smaller risk on faster indices
  • Daily limits protect you from emotions
  • Trade a fixed window, not all day

Module 7 — Your Weekly Plan, Scaling & Mentorship

Consistency comes from a routine you can repeat every week.

Choose and master

Pick 1–2 indices and 1 strategy. Master them for 3 months before adding anything new.

Weekly routine

Sunday: review the journal and mark key levels. Weekdays: trade only your window and your setups. Friday: review results in R and write next week's one improvement.

Scaling safely

Only increase risk after two consecutive profitable months of following rules, and never above 2% per trade.

Support and mentorship

Join the students' WhatsApp group, contact us for one-on-one mentorship and strategy checks, and use the YouTube lessons to revisit any module.

Worked example

Plan: Volatility 25 + Step Index, trade 14:00–16:00, risk 1%, maximum 3 trades per day, review every Sunday. After 8 weeks of following the plan the journal shows an average of +0.3R per trade — a repeatable process.

Key takeaways
  • Master 1–2 indices first
  • Run a weekly review routine
  • Scale slowly and use mentorship for feedback
